About ICEP

A student community at Imperial with a passion for advanced space propulsion.

ICEP brings students from all disciplines at Imperial together to explore electric and deep-space propulsion through practical engineering, theory sessions and shared project work. We are driven by a shared curiosity about how humanity will travel farther into space.

Members develop skills in computer-aided design, manufacturing, assembly, engineering design and Julia programming through team training and hands-on project work. From a first simulation to a part ready for manufacture, there is room to learn, contribute and turn ideas into hardware. No previous Hall-thruster knowledge is required.

Funded by Imperial’s Department of Aeronautics.

Our first project

In development

Hall-effect thruster.

We are developing our first argon Hall-effect thruster, taking it from design and simulation towards manufacture and ground testing.

Materials for the first thruster have been obtained. Through an iterative design process, we are developing the thruster components and supporting systems needed for testing at the Imperial Plasma Propulsion Laboratory.

Project scope

Our first thruster is a practical starting point for a bigger ambition: a lasting student community working on the propulsion, spacecraft and mission concepts that could take exploration beyond Earth.

Thruster development

Work through design reviews, component sizing and procurement, followed by manufacturing, assembly and preparation for a ground demonstration. The work spans the thruster, its supporting systems and the test setup.

Mission & systems studies

Develop conceptual spacecraft missions and use them to define propulsion requirements. Explore spacecraft designs and how thrusters and their supporting systems could scale for future missions.

Student learning & outreach

Build practical experience through technical training, shared research and project work. Grow a community across disciplines and work towards links with other student electric propulsion and CubeSat teams.

Longer-term ambitions

We want ICEP to become a place where students can build the engines of tomorrow and think seriously about the missions they could enable.

Beyond our first thruster

Build on Hall-thruster experience to explore different propellants, gridded-ion engines and electric micro-propulsion for small satellites. Each project should leave the next generation of students with stronger tools, knowledge and hardware.

Humanity beyond Earth

Develop a theoretical research community around human space exploration, conceptual spacecraft and interplanetary missions. We want students from different disciplines to explore what a future spacefaring civilisation could require, from propulsion and power to complete mission architectures.

From ground tests to orbit

Work towards student-built electric propulsion on a CubeSat, whether through our own spacecraft or a future collaboration with another university team. One long-term dream is to work alongside Imperial College London Rocketry on an electrically propelled student CubeSat payload.

A wider student movement

Strengthen links with student teams across the UK, Europe and beyond. Through shared learning, research connections and friendly competition, we hope to help grow opportunities for student electric propulsion projects and future competitions.

Our teams

Find your place in ICEP.

Two main branches, working together on one thruster. Explore the roles below, with part-time Marketing opportunities alongside your technical or simulation work.

ICEP

Design & build

Technical

Turn mission requirements into thruster designs and physical hardware.

Systems and Management

Define a conceptual spacecraft mission and use it to set thruster requirements. Coordinate the subteams, track progress and report our work to stakeholders and academics.

Computer-Aided Design

Size the thruster, develop its conceptual geometry and create detailed 3D models in Fusion 360. Turn engineering requirements into a design the team can manufacture.

Manufacturing, Assembly, Integration and Testing

Develop the magnets, cathode and anode. Manufacture and assemble the hardware, integrate its systems and prepare it for testing.
